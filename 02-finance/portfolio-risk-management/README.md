# Portfolio Risk Management — Geopolitical Stress Review

**Portfolio management · hedging · options overlays · position sizing**

> Investments midterm portfolio discussion, Hult International Business School, March 2026.
> Sole author.

## Problem
A three-holding portfolio — Apple, S&P Global, Palantir — meeting a simultaneous geopolitical
shock: a 15% global baseline tariff, military escalation in the Middle East, and the closure of
the Strait of Hormuz. For each position: what the exposure actually is, and what specific
defensive action follows.

## The three positions

### Apple (AAPL) — tariff exposure on a thin-margin launch
The $599 MacBook Neo is a volume play with thinner margins than the Pro line. A 15% tariff is
roughly **$90 per unit** — absorb it and the product's profitability is gone, pass it on and the
price advantage the product was built around is gone. The Supreme Court ruling that replaced
targeted duties with a global baseline removed the India/Vietnam production workaround, and
Hormuz spiked airfreight and shipping insurance against a launch-day deadline.

**Defence:** covered calls at the **$260–270** strike to harvest premium as a synthetic dividend
against margin compression; a tactical trim if pre-order lead times slip past four weeks, as
that signals supply-chain friction turning a product success into a logistics problem; and
rotation toward Services, which is a non-physical hedge — App Store and iCloud revenue flows
digitally while hardware sits in the Strait.

### S&P Global (SPGI) — a two-sided exposure
SPGI is a toll booth on global capital, so the shock cuts both ways. Geopolitical uncertainty
freezes bond issuance and IPOs, which hits Ratings — the highest-margin division. But Market
Intelligence and Commodity Insights become *more* essential in a crisis, with Brent toward $100
and the tariff regime creating demand for trade-compliance data. Subscription data revenue is a
resilient **85%** of the business, which puts a floor under the stock.

**Defence:** with the stock below its 200-day moving average, a DRIP to accumulate through the
undervaluation; CERAWeek as a catalyst to watch; and a conditional shift of weight toward direct
beneficiaries if the conflict widens, holding SPGI as a core position for the post-conflict
recovery in debt issuance.

### Palantir (PLTR) — the war trade, and the discipline it needs
Palantir is the portfolio's geopolitical hedge and thrives on exactly what hurts Apple — Gotham
and AIP in active DoD use for battlefield intelligence and Gulf maritime tracking, with the
$448M ShipOS deal and the GE Aerospace J85 partnership marking the shift from consultancy to
infrastructure. The risk is valuation: a P/E above **200** and a death cross in February make it
technically fragile, and any de-escalation unwinds the trade fast.

**Defence:** a **10% trailing stop-loss** to keep the upside while capping the reversal, and
treating Palantir as a source of funds — trim on a further 15% spike and rotate into cheaper
value like SPGI.

## The through-line
The three positions are deliberately exposed to the *same* event in opposite directions. Apple
loses on logistics, Palantir gains on conflict, SPGI is hedged internally between Ratings and
Data. That makes Palantir the funding source for rebalancing into Apple and SPGI weakness —
the portfolio recycles the volatility rather than just enduring it, and is positioned for both
continued conflict and a return to peace.
