# Forensic Accounting — Earnings Management and Fraud at TechnoGlobal Inc.

**Forensic analysis · US GAAP (ASC) · restated financials · $141M income overstatement found**

> Team project (4 members) · Financial Reporting, Hult International Business School, March 2026
> TechnoGlobal Inc. is a fictional case company.

## Problem
Review TechnoGlobal's FY2023 annual report and footnotes, identify accounting irregularities,
classify each as fraud or aggressive-but-permitted earnings management, quantify the impact, and
restate the financial statements.

## Findings

Eight red flags. **Three fraudulent, five aggressive earnings management.**

| # | Issue | Standard | Classification | Net income impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Premature revenue recognition (channel stuffing) | ASC 606 | **Fraud** | +$57.2M |
| 2 | Fictitious / uncollectible receivables | ASC 326 | **Fraud** | +$25.3M |
| 3 | Aggressive cut to credit loss allowance | ASC 326 | Earnings mgmt | +$7.2M |
| 4 | Opportunistic FIFO → LIFO switch in Q4 | ASC 250 / 330 | Earnings mgmt | +$10.8M |
| 5 | Failure to write down obsolete inventory | ASC 330 | Earnings mgmt | +$18.1M |
| 6 | PP&E useful life extended 5 → 8 years | ASC 250 / 360 | Earnings mgmt | +$6.0M |
| 7 | Leases reclassified as "consulting contracts" | ASC 842 | **Fraud** | Covenant breach |
| 8 | Aggressive software cost capitalisation | ASC 985-20 | Earnings mgmt | +$16.9M |

### Restated results

| | As reported | Restated | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income | $207M | **$65.6M** | −68% |
| EPS | $4.14 | **$1.31** | −68% |
| Gross margin | 42.0% | 36.1% | −5.9 pts |
| Net margin | 11.2% | 3.7% | −7.5 pts |
| Debt-to-equity | 0.82x | **1.04x** | covenant breach |
| Total assets | — | −$120M | — |

Pre-tax income was overstated by ~$235M; net income by ~$141M.

## The key finding

The most consequential single item is **#7**. TechnoGlobal classified $45M of lease obligations
as "consulting service contracts." Under ASC 842 any arrangement transferring control of an
identified asset is a lease regardless of what it is called.

Capitalising those obligations moves debt-to-equity from 0.82x to **1.04x — through the 1.0x
covenant threshold and into technical default.**

That is what makes this more than a classification error. A D/E of 0.82x sits comfortably inside
the covenant; 1.04x does not. The reclassification was not a routine judgement call — it was
engineered to keep that specific ratio in compliance, and that inference about intent colours
every other adjustment in the report.

## Why the pattern matters more than any single item

Taken alone, each earnings-management item is defensible. The LIFO switch can be argued on
expense matching. The useful-life extension could rest on an internal engineering assessment.
The software capitalisation is an aggressive but survivable reading of ASC 985-20.

The pattern is not defensible. Five favourable estimate changes in a single year, **all in the
same direction**, alongside two recognitions of assets with no economic substance and one
deliberate contract restructuring to avoid covenant disclosure, is not coincidence. It is
systematic.

## Method
Each item was traced to the governing standard, tested against its recognition criteria,
quantified pre- and post-tax, and carried through to restated income statement, balance sheet,
cash flow statement, and ratio analysis.
