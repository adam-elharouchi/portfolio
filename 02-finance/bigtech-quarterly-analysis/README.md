# BigTech — Quarterly Financial Analysis and Projections

**Three-statement analysis · SaaS cohort metrics · annual projections**

> Corporate Finance, Hult International Business School. Case company "Stargaze" is fictional.
> Built on a course-provided workbook shell; the analysis and outputs are mine.

## Problem
Take a subscription business's income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement across
nine quarters (Q1 2021 – Q1 2023) and turn them into the three things an investor actually
wants: how the financials are trending, what the customer base is doing underneath them, and
what next year looks like.

## What the model does

**Quarterly financial overview** — revenues, operating income (EBITDA), net income, net income
per share and free cash flow pulled from the three statements for each quarter, with
quarter-over-quarter growth and the derived metrics: EBITDA margin, net income margin, and FCF
per diluted share.

**Customer cohort analysis** — the part that explains the financials. Beginning users,
attrition, new users, ending users, net change, and **churn rate** per quarter. Revenue trends
in a subscription business are a lagging indicator; churn is the leading one, and separating
gross adds from net change is what shows whether growth is acquisition or retention.

**Annual projections** — FY figures with 2023E annualised from Q1, then 2024E modelled on
revenue +15%, operating income +10%, net income +8%, FCF −2%, with share count and debt held
flat. FCF declining while net income grows is the interesting tension in that scenario: it
implies working capital or capex absorbing the earnings gain, which is exactly the kind of
divergence a margin-only read would miss.

Roughly 1,400 populated values and 500 formulas across seven sheets.

## Deliverable
Findings presented as a deck ([`bigtech-presentation.pptx`](bigtech-presentation.pptx)) —
financial overview, customer trends, and the projections table.
