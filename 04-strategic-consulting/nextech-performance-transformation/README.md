# NexTech Solutions — Strategic Alignment and Performance Transformation

**Balanced scorecard · strategy execution · performance measurement**

## Problem
NexTech enjoyed years of steady growth and then plateaued. The brief treats this as a growth
problem. The analysis argues it is not.

## The diagnosis

**The plateau is a crisis of translation, not ambition.** NexTech has a broad innovation vision
and has failed to convert it into actionable strategic objectives. That gap is what produced the
siloed, short-term-revenue culture — not the other way round.

A vision without strategic boundaries cannot guide resource allocation. When "innovation" is not
pinned to a market domain, a target customer, and a timeline, each department interprets it
through its own narrow lens, and the silos are the predictable result rather than a separate
cultural failing.

## Recommendations

**Redefine the mission around specific customer outcomes**, so innovation serves a function
instead of operating as a slogan. Four strategic imperatives follow:

1. Accelerate innovation throughput
2. Deepen customer-linked product development
3. Ensure enterprise-grade reliability
4. Institutionalise a repeatable innovation system

**Rebuild the performance system** on balanced leading and lagging indicators (Kaplan & Norton,
1992), rather than the lagging financial measures that currently drive behaviour. Lagging
indicators tell you the plateau happened; they cannot tell you it is coming.

## Why the measurement argument carries the case
The temptation with a plateaued firm is to recommend a new strategy. The stronger argument is
that NexTech's existing ambition is fine and its *measurement system* is selecting against it —
if the only metrics that carry weight are short-term financial returns, then a rational manager
defunds long-horizon innovation regardless of what the vision statement says. Changing the
scorecard changes the behaviour; changing the slogan does not.
