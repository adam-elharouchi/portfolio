# Café Nero — Customer Experience Field Study

**Jobs-to-Be-Done · structured field observation · service design**

## Problem
How do customers actually behave in a mid-scale coffee chain inside a busy mall, and what in the
environment and the service process is quietly costing the brand repeat visits?

## Method

Field research at the Café Nero inside Boston's Prudential Center, structured rather than casual:

- **Two time slots** — morning (8–10 a.m.) and midday (12–1 p.m.), on separate weekdays
- **~45 minutes per session**, observing entry, ordering, seating, and departure
- **Three informal patron interviews** for qualitative colour
- Analysed through a **Jobs-to-Be-Done** lens plus environmental behaviour theory
  (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974)

**JTBD statement:** *"The customer is trying to secure a quick, reliable coffee and snack
experience that fits seamlessly into a fast-paced daily routine."*

## Finding
Café Nero succeeds at the functional job — it is a dependable stop for commuters and mall
workers. Behind that functional success sit design and service bottlenecks that cap the
relationship at convenience and prevent deeper brand attachment.

That distinction is the point of the study. A satisfaction survey would score this location well,
because customers are getting the job done. Observing the process rather than asking about it is
what surfaces the friction they have stopped noticing and would never think to report.
