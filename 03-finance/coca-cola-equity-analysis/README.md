# Equity Analysis — The Coca-Cola Company (KO)

**Comparables · Dividend Discount Model · market sentiment · three methods, three answers**

> Asset Valuation & Financial Analysis (FIN-0305), Hult International Business School,
> December 2025. Team project, 4 members.

## Problem
Value KO using multiple independent methods and reconcile the results — a "quality compounder"
trading at a premium to its peer group is exactly the case where different methods disagree.

## Method 1 — Comparables

Peer set: PepsiCo (PEP), Keurig Dr Pepper (KDP), Monster Beverage (MNST).

| Finding | |
|---|---|
| KO multiples vs. peer average | **15–25% above** |
| Largest gap | P/S — investors paying ~3x the peer level |
| Dividend yield | ~2.7% vs. peers' 3.2–3.6% (lowest among dividend payers) |
| EPS growth | Slower than PEP, KDP, and MNST |
| Implied fair value (peer P/E × KO EPS) | **$71–72** vs. market $71.95 |
| Implied value (peer EV/EBITDA × KO EBITDA) | Above market price |

**Read:** investors are paying a quality-and-safety premium for brand equity, global
diversification, and ~60 consecutive years of dividend increases — not for superior growth.
Fairly valued on earnings; modestly undervalued on a cash-flow basis.

## Method 2 — Dividend Discount Model

Constant-growth DDM, required return 7%, sustainable dividend growth 5.87%:

| | |
|---|---|
| Intrinsic value | **$180–190** |
| Market price | ~$70.50 |
| Expected total return | 8.9% (3.1% yield + 5.9% growth) |

**This result should not be taken at face value, and the report says so.** A $180–190 intrinsic
value against a $70.50 market price is not a 150% buying opportunity — it is a warning that the
model is being used outside its reliable range. With r = 7% and g = 5.87%, the denominator
(r − g) is 1.13%. At that spread the output is hypersensitive: shifting the required return by a
single point roughly halves the valuation. The honest conclusion is that the constant-growth DDM
is the wrong tool for a mature firm whose sustainable growth rate sits this close to its cost of
equity, not that the market has mispriced KO by a factor of two and a half.

## Method 3 — Market sentiment

| | |
|---|---|
| Analyst consensus | Buy / Strong Buy |
| Average 12-month target | $78–80 |
| Implied upside | ~10–13% plus dividend |
| Example | Bank of America raised target $78 → $80, maintaining Buy |

Sources: TipRanks, MarketBeat, published bank research.

## Reconciliation

Three methods, three different answers — comparables say fairly valued, DDM says deeply
undervalued, the street says 10–13% upside. The comparables and sentiment estimates cluster in
the $71–80 range and corroborate each other. The DDM is the outlier, and its disagreement is
explained by its own sensitivity rather than by an insight the market has missed. Weighting the
evidence by method reliability rather than averaging the outputs, KO reads as fairly valued with
modest upside.
