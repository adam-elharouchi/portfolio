# AeroShield — Operations Management Capstone

**Throughput and cycle-time analysis · bottleneck identification · capacity planning**

> Operations Management capstone, Hult International Business School, Spring 2026.
> Team project; the deck and throughput model in this folder are my own work.

## Problem
AeroShield Materials manufactures aerogel-insulated glass units (IGUs). The company was moving
from a low-volume pilot line to industrial-scale production, and needed an operational diagnostic:
where the bottleneck sits, what throughput the proposed line can actually sustain, and what has
to change to hit the target.

## My deliverables

**[Operations portfolio deck](aeroshield-operations-portfolio.pptx)** — 10 slides covering:

1. 4Vs operational analysis (volume, variety, variation, visibility)

3. End-to-end process map and process-type classification

## Approach

The operational profile sits at **high volume / low variety / low variation / low visibility** —
the left side of the 4Vs spectrum, characteristic of standardised batch process manufacturing.
That classification is what justifies capital-intensive automation and high equipment
utilisation over flexibility.

The analysis is built around **cycle time per stage**, because in a batch chemical process the
stage with the longest cycle sets the rate for the entire line regardless of how fast every other
stage runs. Adding capacity anywhere other than the binding constraint buys nothing. The model
compares two operating phases stage by stage to locate that constraint, then sizes the
recommendations against it — which is why the leading technology recommendation targets the
vessel stage specifically rather than proposing general capacity increases.
